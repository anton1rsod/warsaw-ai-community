## 4. The work

Members come to the community for three reasons:

1. **Sharpening ideas.** Pre-formed and half-formed ideas get pressure-tested by professionals from adjacent industries who can see weaknesses the founder cannot. The cross-industry composition is the engine here.
2. **Matching partners.** Finding co-founders, advisors, design help, engineering help, capital, customers. The cross-industry membership means the right human is usually one introduction away.
3. **Building together.** Joint sub-projects, shared infrastructure, joint commercial bets where multiple members co-own the upside.

The community produces durable artifacts: the platform, the knowledge base, the playbooks, the architecture decision records. New members inherit a stack; departing members leave behind what they built. Continuity matters more than turnover.

## 5. The venture studio that this is becoming

### Why a studio, not a fund

A fund writes checks. A studio is an operating company that incubates and partially owns the ventures it builds. The studio model fits this community because:

- Members are already building together
- The community already has shared infrastructure (platform, knowledge base, network) that can support new ventures
- The members want active partnership, not passive capital
- The founder is an operator, not a check-writer

### The mechanism

The studio will:
- Co-build new ventures alongside member-founders
- Bring shared infrastructure to those ventures (platform, knowledge, network, design support, engineering support)
- Take partial equity in ventures it co-builds
- Stay actively involved in operations, not just board observation

### Why the community wrapper today

The community wrapper exists today for legal and regulatory simplicity — to avoid being mistaken as a licensed fund or registered investment vehicle while the studio model is being built and tested. The wrapper does not change the underlying intent. The intent is **studio operations**; the community is the front door.

### Why the same name across phases

A rebrand from a community brand to a studio brand would cost trust, momentum, and the substantial investment we have already made in the community brand and platform. **The name we pick today needs to be the name on the cap table tomorrow.** One name, two phases.

### Timeline

Commercial-track, accelerated. The community wrapper is real and stable today. The studio formalization is a 6–18 month horizon. The name needs to be live and defensible before the studio formalization.

## 9. Naming criteria

### Must

- 1–2 words; one word strongly preferred
- 4–8 letters total
- ASCII only — no diacritics or special characters
- Pronounceable globally — English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Polish, Mandarin speakers all say it the same way
- 1–3 syllables (1–2 strongly preferred)
- Intuitive spelling — does not need to be explained when spoken aloud
- Distinctive — does not collide with a major existing brand in venture capital, AI, professional networks, or general consumer brand
- Acquirable `.com` domain (free, or premium under reasonable cost — see §10)

### Should

- Be either a single distinctive word or a coined-short invented word — any language root is acceptable provided global pronounceability holds
- Sit naturally next to the existing visual identity
- Scale gracefully from "community" framing to "venture studio" brand without further rebrand
- Carry a quality of creation, building, growth, ignition, momentum, ascent, or emergence — without literally being any of those words

### Must NOT (the only categorical exclusions — beyond these you have full latitude)

- **Slavic / Polish vocabulary** — categorically excluded by founder direction. Ambition is international; the founder does not want geographic encoding through the name.
- **Geographic references** — no Warsaw, Poland, Vistula, any city, country, region, or landmark.
- **IT or tech-coded vocabulary** — no AI, ML, code, dev, build, ship, stack, sync, ops, cloud, byte, bit, lab, hub, or any other word that reads as "this is a tech company". The brand is cross-industry; tech vocabulary locks it.
- **Literal suffix labels** — no Studio, Lab, Hub, Builder, Founders, Ventures, Capital, Fund, Group, Network, Collective, Forum, Co. The name should stand on its own.

Beyond these four categories, **you have full freedom** to explore any language root, any aesthetic cluster, any naming pattern that serves the brief.

## 12. Research method

Verify, don't guess.

- **Domains:** real whois / GoDaddy / Namecheap / Domainr lookups, not extrapolation from search results. For premium domains, real price quotes if your tools allow.
- **Trademarks:** USPTO TESS, EUIPO eSearch, WIPO Global Brand Database at minimum.
- **Web search:** for each top-10 candidate run `"[name] venture studio"`, `"[name] community"`, `"[name] startup"`, and `"[name]" site:linkedin.com/company`.
- **Wikipedia:** is `[name]` primarily disambiguated to an existing brand or to a general dictionary entry?
- **Phonetic collision:** for each top-3, check phonetic neighbors in EN / FR / DE / ES / IT / PL / ZH.

**Confidence calibration:** if you're uncertain about a fact (domain status, trademark presence, brand existence), say so. I would rather see 8 well-verified candidates than 15 sloppily checked ones.
