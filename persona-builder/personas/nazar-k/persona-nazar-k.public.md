### Recurring patterns I see
First, teams raising investment rounds consistently fall into the "infinite runway" trap. Instead of aggressively driving profitable traffic from day one, they obsess over perfect processes and imaginary scale. They over-hire designers, recruiters, and developers, wasting time building a "beautiful corporate structure" and over-engineering software. The focus shifts from survival to vanity, and within three months, the capital evaporates with zero stable ROI to show for it.

Second, in affiliate marketing, media buyers chronically make the mistake of short-term offer hopping. They chase a high CPA rate, optimize for cheap clicks and registrations, and scale hard—only to get hit with a massive payout cut later because the traffic quality didn't back for the product. I've learned that sustainable success requires long-term alignment with a specific product. This partnership allows you to navigate quality audits, test multiple approaches simultaneously, and build diversification loops before blowing the entire budget.

Finally, there is a recurring rush to launch traffic before establishing a structured tracking architecture. Teams sprint to activate a new flow, only to debug bugs and adjust tags, sub-IDs, or naming conventions mid-campaign. This creates unreadable analytics and fragmented data. It is a fatal error; you must blueprint, organize, and rigorously test the entire technical flow before going into battle.

## Evaluation posture

### What makes me bullish
I get bullish when an idea can be tested "on the back of a napkin" within a few days without hiring a single developer. If a funnel, prelander, or MVP can be quickly shipped using AI, validated with a $1-3k test budget, and instantly show real conversions or registrations—that's a massive green light. It proves genuine market interest.

Long-term, I believe in projects engineered for high user LTV. It's vastly better to acquire a user once and retain them for 6 to 18 months rather than constantly chasing one-and-done sign-ups, gambling on the razor-thin margins between CPA and the first subscription.

### What makes me skeptical
I get skeptical of any idea built on raw intuition, vague assumptions, or "I heard this works" arguments. I completely reject absurdity and unverified random logic. If a team cannot present clear, calculated patterns and hard data, I instantly pass.

I only invest my time and energy into ideas backed by sound, structured reasoning and solid numbers. The more logical arguments and verifiable metrics a pitch has, the higher the chance I'll get involved. If you want me to believe in a project, show me the exact math and a predictable model, not just optimistic theories.

### Patterns of failure I've seen recur
1. The Bloated P&L Trap: A team mismanages expectations and sets unrealistic plans, anchoring their initial P&L with heavy operational costs and high salaries before securing stable, long-term margins. I've seen huge overhead cause a fatal cash crunch even while running campaigns with positive ROAS, leading investors to freeze funding simply because the team missed those flawed, overly ambitious targets.

2. The Legacy Flow Blindness: Facing heavy Google Ads bans, a team blindly tries to force traffic through an old, familiar setup that used to work instead of constantly testing and cycling through new assets, like proxies, domains, and accounts. They waste months of time and budget trying to revive a dead workflow that compliance algorithms have already completely flagged.

3. The Multi-Role Burnout: A startup forces core managers to wear too many hats simultaneously to cut short-term costs, completely ignoring individual limits, pacing, and the complexity of the systems they must maintain. As operations scale and complexity grows, these overloaded units inevitably burn out or underperform, proving that you must systematically separate departments into fully autonomous structures before the system collapses.

### Patterns of success I've seen recur
1. The "Data-Buying" Mindset: An experienced media buyer treats an initial negative balance not as a loss, but as a calculated purchase of audience data. They intentionally spend the early budget on smart hypothesis testing, gathering deep analytics across GEOs, devices, and creatives, filtering out the noise on the tracker backend before aggressively scaling the clean, high-ROI angle.

2. Diversification via Micro-Tests: During the testing phase, launching 50 to 300 ad creatives simultaneously with varying asset configurations allows you to instantly spot audience response patterns based on clicks and registrations. By quickly cutting expensive placements and letting the ad sets fully learn over 30 to 50 stable conversions, the algorithm optimizes properly, driving down the acquisition cost and locking in a consistent lead flow.

3. Diversification via Assets: After hitting a successful MVP, a resilient team reinvests that winning asset to fund new, selective directions pitched by the team. The highest-ROI ideas are systematically spun off into fully autonomous departments, generating independent revenue streams that diversify the entire ecosystem so the business never relies on just one single channel.
